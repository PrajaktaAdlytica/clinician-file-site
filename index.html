11 / A few important distinctions

Clear before
the first conversation.

Is this a working credentialing product?

No. This is a research-stage concept and synthetic product preview. There are no demonstrated customers, live integrations, demand, revenue, pricing acceptance, or validated outcomes.

Are credentialing, privileging, and enrollment the same?

No. Credentialing evaluates professional qualifications; privileging concerns permission to perform specific services within an organization; payer enrollment concerns participation and administrative enrollment with a payer. The preview tracks them separately and never turns one status into another.

Does ClinicianFile connect to CAQH, PECOS, or licensing boards?

No present access is claimed. Integration is a discovery question. Each source needs an authorized route—licensed, permitted API, or human/manual access—and applicable terms must be reviewed.

What is included in the first pilot?

The first proposed module is the provider dossier, missing-document queue, and expiry view. A governed pilot follows the readiness gates, written scope, owner, security/data-access review, and human oversight approvals.

Will an agent approve or submit on a clinician’s behalf?

No automated determinations or approval authority are assumed. Credentialing specialists verify sources and make authorized human submissions. The preview records fictional ownership and unsent states.

What should we share for the audit conversation?

Your role, organization, region, approximate provider count, relevant portals, and where rework occurs. Do not submit provider credentials, patient information, clinical details, or records through this preview.
